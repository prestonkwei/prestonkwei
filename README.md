I build AI agents, and then I rebuild them.

It started with an assistant that read an inbox and booked meetings on its own. Then one that places outbound calls and holds a real speech-to-speech conversation. Then one that ordered a pizza and paid with a single-use virtual card, mostly to find out where an agent breaks when it has to touch something real.

Every version fails somewhere new. That's the useful part.

Most of my time now goes a layer below the agents themselves: the memory and context services they read from, the messaging gateways they act through, and the pipelines that let them run on their own instead of when I ask.

I also work on identity and auth, developer tooling, and internal platforms that a handful of people depend on daily.

Mostly I just like to build things.
